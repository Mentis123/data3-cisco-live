## How the 90 minutes run

Your deck, unchanged in content. Six rounds: Triage, then Injects 1–5. Teams of 2–3, lots of tables.

Before slide 10, Adam presses **Launch** and the projector plays the attacker run from your Crisis Room — the adversary dials are fixed to the scenario (encrypt / exfiltrate / extort · exposed AI agent · fully autonomous, mirroring JADEPUFFER) so the launch always matches the deck. Phones show Riverbend's systems in monitoring mode; the moment the launch happens, "something tripped a faint signal".

Each round, in the order we agreed on the call:

1. Situation slide goes up; Adam opens the round. Phones show the incoming feed, the situation lines and A–D **in the deck's order** with the deck's wording.
2. Four-minute clock on the projector. Tables argue, pick A–D, write one sentence on why, pick confidence 1–5, **lock**.
3. Adam **closes the round**. Nothing is revealed yet.
4. You walk the option slide. The build is now fixed on all six option slides: row A → B → C → D on click, each with its label, and the **best-move box appears on the final click** as the punchline.
5. Adam **shows the split**. Projector: A/B/C/D bars, confidence range, up to two rationales Adam picked (unattributed). Every phone shows *that table's* consequence and its meters move — ported from your Crisis Room, including the branches (a table that put eyes on the OT gateway in Inject 1 rides out the isolation in Inject 3; a table that kept a human override in Inject 2 keeps its insurer cover in Inject 4).
6. Discussion. Then Next.

Then your debrief, defences, six moves, self-assessment and close, as in the deck.

## Decisions we made so nobody's blocked

- **Split after the slide, not before.** Adam's point from the call: if the whole room voted A and we then say C, they feel marked wrong. So: walk the options, reveal the best move, *then* show the split and discuss. Your original instinct (split first) is one button-press away if you'd rather on the day.
- **Counts, not names.** The projector shows how many tables chose each option, never which table. Adam sees names on his private page so he can pick who to ask.
- **One launch, on the big screen, matched to the scenario** — rather than every table building a different attacker that wouldn't match the slides.
- **Consequences only after the split.** The Crisis Room showed the consequence the moment you clicked; we hold it until the split so your punchline on the slide lands first.
- **Four minutes per round, advisory.** Nothing closes automatically; Adam decides.
- **No scores, no ranking, no winner anywhere.** Same principle as the pack.
- **Microsoft slide and the CISO Basecamp framing stay as you have them.**

## What we need from you

- If you want any option wording changed, change it in the deck and tell us — the phones mirror the slides word for word, so the two must move together.
